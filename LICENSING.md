This is a working summary for the people building GNARL, not legal advice.
Get a real opinion before selling it.

## What is NOT GPLv3

These are separate programs. They are not linked into the plugin and do not
derive from Vital, so they keep their own terms.

| Path | What |
|---|---|
| `site/` | the marketing site |
| `backend/` | the Cloudflare Worker for licence activation |
| `docs/` | project documents |

**Presets and wavetables are data, not code.** A preset pack shipped as
separate files, not compiled into the binary, can carry its own licence. If
presets are ever embedded with `juce_add_binary_data`, that stops being true
for the embedded ones.

## What GPLv3 obliges us to do

When we distribute a binary to anyone - including a paying customer:

1. **Offer the complete corresponding source** for that exact binary, under
   GPLv3: every change, every riddim feature, and the build scripts.
2. **Add no further restrictions.** No EULA term that forbids redistribution;
   it would be void against the GPL anyway.
3. **Keep the notices.** `LICENSE`, every per-file copyright header, and the
   attribution in `README.md` stay intact.
4. **Mark modified files** as modified, with a date - GPLv3 §5(a). This
   repository's history from the merge commit onward is that record, and
   [CHANGES.md](CHANGES.md) summarises it.

The consequence worth stating plainly: **a customer who receives GNARL may
lawfully give it to anyone, for free.** GPLv3 permits charging; it does not
permit exclusivity. Upstream offers non-GPL licensing at
`licensing@vital.audio` for anyone who needs that, and a JUCE commercial
licence would then be needed too.
